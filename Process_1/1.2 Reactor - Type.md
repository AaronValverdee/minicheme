## 1. Preliminary Engineering Design Proposal

A **Two-Stage Fixed-Bed Catalytic Reactor Train with Intermediate Cooling** is selected for MTBE synthesis:

* **Primary Reactor (R-101)**: Vertical fixed-bed reactor operating in liquid downflow over a packed bed of macroporous sulfonic acid resin beads. Converts $\approx 85-90\%$ of isobutylene. Operating inlet $50^\circ\text{C}$, exiting at $\le 75^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Interstage Cooler (E-102)**: Shell-and-tube heat exchanger cooling the reaction mixture from $75^\circ\text{C}$ down to $45-50^\circ\text{C}$ using cooling water.
* **Finishing / Polishing Reactor (R-102)**: Second adiabatic fixed-bed catalytic reactor operating at lower temperature ($45^\circ\text{C} \to 55^\circ\text{C}$), shifting the exothermic thermodynamic equilibrium to achieve **$>97-99\%$ overall isobutylene conversion**.
* **Catalyst**: **Amberlyst 15** (or Purolite CT-175 / Dowex M-31) strongly acidic macroporous cation-exchange resin (sulfonated crosslinked styrene-divinylbenzene copolymer) in spherical bead form ($d_p = 0.6 - 0.8\text{ mm}$).

## 2. First-Principles Rationale & Selection Analysis

### 2.1 PFR vs. CSTR Volume Comparison
The rate of MTBE formation follows Langmuir-Hinshelwood / positive-order kinetics with respect to isobutylene:
$$r = \frac{k \cdot a_{i\text{-C4}} \cdot a_{\text{MeOH}}}{1 + K_{\text{MeOH}} \cdot a_{\text{MeOH}}}$$

Because reaction rate decreases monotonically with reactant conversion:
$$\frac{V_{\text{CSTR}}}{V_{\text{PFR}}} = \frac{\frac{X}{1-X}}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-X}\right)}$$

For $X = 90\%$ single-pass conversion:
$$\frac{V_{\text{CSTR}}}{V_{\text{PFR}}} = \frac{\frac{0.90}{0.10}}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{0.10}\right)} = \frac{9.0}{2.303} \approx 3.91$$

A CSTR cascade would require nearly $4\times$ the catalyst volume of a plug-flow fixed-bed. Therefore, a **fixed-bed tubular plug-flow reactor (PFR)** provides maximum catalyst efficiency and minimum vessel volume (`REACT-003`).

### 2.2 Why Two Adiabatic Stages with Intercooling vs. Single Multi-tubular Exchanger
For the plant design throughput ($1,250\text{ kg/h}$ total naphtha, or $\approx 2.2\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$ liquid volumetric flow):
1. **Multi-tubular Exchanger Reactor**: High capital fabrication cost for small tube bundles, complex catalyst loading/unloading into hundreds of small $1''-1.5''$ tubes, and difficult flow maldistribution in small tubes.
2. **Two-Stage Adiabatic Train**: Two simple cylindrical pressure vessels (R-101 and R-102) with an external standard shell-and-tube cooler (E-102) between them.
   - Stage 1 achieves fast kinetics at $50-75^\circ\text{C}$ and removes $85\%$ of the total reaction heat.
   - Stage 2 takes advantage of the higher equilibrium constant $K_{\text{eq}}$ at lower temperature ($45-55^\circ\text{C}$), driving final conversion past $98\%$ without exceeding catalyst thermal limits.

### 2.3 Evaluation of Reactive Distillation (CDTech Route)
Heuristic `PROC-008` recommends evaluating reactive separations for equilibrium-limited reactions:
* **Industrial Context**: Catalytic distillation (CDTech / CDMtbe) is the premier technology for mega-scale refineries ($>100,000\text{ t/year}$ MTBE) to reach $99.5\%$ conversion in a single tower.
* **Scale Evaluation**: At $10,000\text{ t/year}$ naphtha ($2,950\text{ t/year}$ MTBE product), proprietary licensed catalytic packing (e.g. catalyst bales/wire gauze envelopes) incurs high licensing fees, mechanical complexity, and maintenance overhead for a small diameter column.
* **Conclusion**: The two-stage fixed bed with conventional debutizer fractionation delivers identical product purity ($>99.9\text{ wt}\%$) and $>98\%$ conversion with standard off-the-shelf equipment and proven commercial resin beads.
