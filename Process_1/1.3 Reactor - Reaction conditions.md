## 2. First-Principles Rationale & Hydraulics

### 2.1 Bubble Point & Pressure Determination
The liquid feed is a multi-component hydrocarbon-alcohol mixture dominated by isobutane ($35\text{ wt}\%$, $T_b = -11.7^\circ\text{C}$), 2-butenes ($21\text{ wt}\%$, $T_b \approx 2^\circ\text{C}$), isobutylene ($19\text{ wt}\%$, $T_b = -6.9^\circ\text{C}$), and methanol ($T_b = 64.7^\circ\text{C}$).
* At the peak reactor temperature of $75^\circ\text{C}$, the bubble point pressure of this mixture is:
  $$P_{\text{bubble}}(75^\circ\text{C}) \approx \sum x_i \gamma_i P^\circ_{\text{vap}, i}(75^\circ\text{C}) \approx 7.2\text{ bar abs} = 6.2\text{ barg}$$
* **Safety Margin**: Setting the operating pressure to **$12.0\text{ barg}$ ($13.0\text{ bar abs}$)** provides an overpressure margin of $\Delta P_{\text{subcooled}} \approx 5.8\text{ bar}$. This guarantees:
  1. Complete liquid-phase operation throughout the catalyst bed.
  2. Prevention of localized boiling or hot spots that could damage the resin polymer matrix.
  3. Stable, pulsation-free downflow percolation through the porous bead bed.

### 2.2 Feed Pumping Hydraulics (`PROC-009`, `PUMP-001`)
* **Feed Conditions at Battery Limit**: Naphtha arrives at $4.0\text{ barg}$ and $25^\circ\text{C}$. Green methanol arrives at $0.0\text{ barg}$ ($1.0\text{ bar abs}$) and $25^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Feed Boost Pumps (P-101 A/B & P-102 A/B)**:
  * Naphtha Pump (P-101): Boosts $1,250\text{ kg/h}$ ($2.12\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$) from $4\text{ barg}$ to $13.5\text{ barg}$ ($\Delta P = 9.5\text{ bar} = 950\text{ kPa}$).
  * Methanol Pump (P-102): Boosts $142.4\text{ kg/h}$ ($0.18\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$) from $0\text{ barg}$ to $13.5\text{ barg}$ ($\Delta P = 13.5\text{ bar}$).
  * **Hydraulic Power Calculation** (`PUMP-002`):
    $$P_{\text{hyd, P-101}} = \frac{\dot{V} \cdot \Delta P}{3600} = \frac{(2.12\text{ m}^3\text{/h}) \cdot (950\text{ kPa})}{3600} = 0.56\text{ kW}$$
    With a pump efficiency $\eta \approx 60\%$, motor driver rating is $1.1\text{ kW}$ (operating load $\approx 0.93\text{ kW}$).
  * Pumping liquid requires $<1\text{ kW}$ of electrical power, confirming heuristic `PROC-009` (pressurize liquids via pumps rather than vaporizing/compressing gases).

### 2.3 Reactor Bed Hydraulics & Ergun Equation
For spherical resin particles with $d_p = 0.75\text{ mm}$ and bed voidage $\varepsilon = 0.38$:
* Cross-sectional area of R-101 ($D = 0.60\text{ m}$): $A_c = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.60)^2 = 0.283\text{ m}^2$.
* Superficial liquid velocity: $u_s = \frac{2.28\text{ m}^3\text{/h}}{3600 \cdot 0.283\text{ m}^2} = 0.00224\text{ m/s}$.
* Liquid density $\rho \approx 600\text{ kg/m}^3$, viscosity $\mu \approx 0.16\text{ mPa}\cdot\text{s} = 1.6 \times 10^{-4}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.
* Modified Reynolds number: $Re_p = \frac{\rho u_s d_p}{\mu(1-\varepsilon)} = \frac{600 \cdot 0.00224 \cdot 0.00075}{1.6 \times 10^{-4} \cdot 0.62} \approx 10.2$ (laminar/transition).
* Applying the Ergun equation yields $\frac{\Delta P}{L} \approx 1.8\text{ kPa/m}$. For $L = 2.12\text{ m}$, bed friction pressure drop is $\Delta P_{\text{bed}} \approx 3.8\text{ kPa} \approx 0.04\text{ bar}$, well within the structural crushing strength of Amberlyst 15 resin beads ($\Delta P_{\max} \approx 1.5\text{ bar}$).

## 3. Justifying Heuristic Rules

| Heuristic ID | Heuristic Rule Summary | Key Guidelines & Equations Applied | Source Reference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `REACT-002` | Reaction rate & operating window | Reaction conditions must respect catalyst stability bounds. Maintain $T \le 75^\circ\text{C}$ to avoid sulfonic resin desulfonation occurring at $>120^\circ\text{C}$. | Turton (2012) Table 11.17 |
| `PROC-009` | Liquid pumping vs. gas compression | Pressurize feeds as liquids before entering reactors. Pumping liquids requires $<1\text{ kW}$, saving massive electrical energy compared to gas compression. | Seider (2010) Heuristic 44 |
| `PUMP-001` | Pump type selection | Flow rate of $2.3\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$ ($10\text{ gpm}$) at $9.5\text{ bar}$ head ($160\text{ m}$): specify multi-stage centrifugal or regenerative turbine pump. | Ulrich & Vasudevan (2004) p. 583 |
| `PIPE-001` | Economic liquid pipe sizing | Size liquid feed piping for economic velocity $u = 1.0 - 2.0\text{ m/s}$. For $2.3\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$, a $1''$ schedule 40 pipe ($ID = 26.6\text{ mm}$) yields $u = 1.15\text{ m/s}$. | Sinnott & Towler (2013) Table 5.2 |
